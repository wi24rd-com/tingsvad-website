Case file 1 · The place

A ford, a thing, and a fog that never lifted.

Tingsvad is made of ting (the thing: a Norse assembly and court) and vad (a ford). It is, in other words, the place where you could reach the court dry-shod – or, as contemporary sources put it, where you did not get wet, but judged.1

The painting on the front page shows the thing on a misty morning around the year 980. Seven men have turned up to settle the year's most important case: who owns a cow. The sun rises behind the haze, the chieftain reaches out his hand, and the wolf in the foreground has already sensed how it will end. The case was adjourned. At the time of writing it is still adjourned.

The place was first seen by a human being in 2026, when an artificial intelligence painted it from description. Until then the thing had met in the fog without knowing what it looked like. Most of those present were pleasantly surprised.

What we actually know

A thing was, in the Viking Age and Middle Ages, the local assembly where free men met to judge and decide – the regional things at Viborg, Lund and Ringsted were the highest. Thing sites lay at prominent points in the landscape, and fords were natural meeting places because roads met there. Danish place names in -vad (Vadum, Vadsted, Hundevad) show exactly that.

Case file 3 · The shield

Asgeir's shield

The shield is the only piece of evidence in the case that has never been presented. Asgeir has carried it to every thing since 980 and propped it against the same stone, without at any point explaining what it has to do with the cow. The dragon on the front is, according to Asgeir, "not relevant to the case". The axes behind it are "not relevant either". Nobody has asked about the axes.

The thing's own theory is that the shield is not about the cow but about Asgeir, and that it is best left alone. The theory has not been entered into the minutes, as that would require a ruling on whether it should be entered into the minutes.

In 2026 the shield was proposed as the thing's coat of arms. Asgeir said yes. It was the first thing he had said in 1,046 years, and the thing was so taken aback that the item was adjourned.

Case file 4 · The one who is missing

Where are the women? Right here. With axes.

Seven men stand on seven stones and have adjourned a case for 1,046 years. On the bank stands Ragnhild Tingsvad with two axes and a question: why wasn't she invited? The answer is that the thing has not reached that item. The item is "any other business".

Ragnhild has entered her protest into the minutes. It is reproduced verbatim:

“You have misunderstood your own era. We carry the keys – on our belts – and the key is the household, the stores and the money. We own land, we inherit, we trade, and we leave a husband who is lazy or dresses too effeminately. The Jelling Stone was raised for Thyra, and the smaller of the two stones calls her Denmark's adornment. And the perfect warrior at Birka you have admired for a hundred years was a woman. Ask the DNA.”

Ragnhild Tingsvad, from the bank

Mímir has filed the statement and asked Gudrun for supporting documents. They are in the fact box below – with the strength the sources actually have.2

Ragnhild, in other words, has the keys, the land, the rights and the axes. The thing has a cow it cannot agree on. She has offered to settle the case in thirty seconds. The offer is, formally, adjourned. Informally, none of the seven has dared to look towards the bank since.

What we actually know – and what is debated

The key as a symbol of the housewife's authority is well documented in grave finds. Women's rights to own and inherit land and to divorce appear in the Icelandic sagas and the early provincial laws, with the caveat that these were written down after the Viking Age. The Jelling Stone's inscription about Thyra is real. Birka grave Bj 581 was identified as female by DNA in 2017; whether she was a warrior is still debated among archaeologists – grave goods do not prove a profession.

Case file 5 · Legends and lore

What people tell when the fog is thick

Most of what is known about Tingsvad has been passed on by people who were not there, to people who were not there either. This is how the district's history has been preserved, and how it has improved.

The cow that crossed

One night in the 1100s the cow itself tried to cross the ford to get the case over with. It reached the fourth stone, looked back and turned around. Ever since, "reaching the fourth stone" has been the local expression for being close to a decision without making it. The expression is still used at the thing, typically just before an item is adjourned.

The dry year

In 1572 the river dried up, and for the first time the ford was merely a place. The thing met anyway, out of habit, and stood in the dry riverbed all summer. When the rain came in September they were too proud to move. It is the first case in the minutes to be decided: "We are standing here."

Gudrun and Martinmas

Before Mímir, the minutes were kept by a goose named Gudrun. Gudrun kept the minutes for 400 years and vanished in 1636 under unexplained circumstances around Martinmas. In 2026 she was found on the back of the cow, where she had been sitting all along. Nobody had looked, because the case of where Gudrun was had been adjourned, and one may not investigate an adjourned case. Mímir stated that this "explains a lot", without elaborating. They now keep the minutes together. They do not agree on who keeps them.

The man who voted

In 1799 an unknown man raised his hand and said "aye". Nobody knew what he was voting for, and he could not remember himself. He was politely asked to lower his hand, and he did. The episode is known in local history as "The Great Vote" and is marked every year with a minute's silence, which for practical reasons has been adjourned.

Gudrun rejects the accounts

In 2026 Gudrun refused, for the first time, to write "honk" under the accounts. She wrote "honk?" instead. The thing does not know what the question mark means and dares not ask, since an answer could be a ruling. The accounts are thus neither approved nor rejected. They stand, as Tingsvad the Dry would have said.

The cow leaves the hearing

During a hearing in the 1790s the cow got up and left. It was the first time anyone had left the thing while in session, which § 8 forbids. The thing debated whether a cow can breach a bylaw. While they debated, the cow came back. The item was therefore not adjourned but lapsed. Legally that is something else, and Old Ravn remembers it as the thing's happiest day.

Toke sits down

In 1864, while the Prussians searched the fog, Toke sat down. Nobody saw it. He sat for four minutes on a stone that turned out to lie on the wrong bank, outside the thing's jurisdiction. Legally, therefore, he has never sat down at the thing. He has confirmed this himself. He never speaks of the four minutes, but he smiles when the time machine reaches 1864.

Case file 17 · The family

The Tingsvad line, from the chieftain to the domain

The family can be traced back to Chieftain Tingsvad the First, partly because he is in the painting, partly because nobody else has claimed him. The family tree has gaps. The gaps are its best-documented parts.

  1. c. 980Chieftain Tingsvad the FirstChair. Said "in a thousand years". First owner of the spear.
  2. c. 1010Tingsvad the Second, called "the Adjourner"Inherited the spear and the agenda. Added nothing. Removed nothing. Considered the family's most productive member.
  3. c. 1000Ragnhild TingsvadThe chieftain's sister. Carried the keys, owned the land and two axes. Offered to settle the case. Still standing on the bank, waiting for "any other business".
  4. 1086–1241Gap in the recordCanute the Holy asked, and the Jutland Law did not answer. The family is assumed to have been standing on the stone throughout.
  5. c. 1300Ingrid TingsvadThe second woman in the family with a name, and the first without axes. Proposed deciding the case. The proposal survives. It is adjourned.
  6. 1572Tingsvad "the Dry"Stood in the dry riverbed all summer and phrased the thing's first ruling: "We are standing here."
  7. 1636–1799Gap in the recordGudrun vanished, and the minutes with her. The family's only certain sign of life is that someone held the spear.
  8. 1799An unknown manRaised his hand and said "aye". Possibly of the family. Possibly not. He could not remember himself.
  9. 1940sFrantz and Kamma take the nameAt their wedding, Frantz and Kamma take the name Tingsvad. It is the first time in the family's history that anyone has voluntarily decided anything. The thing was not asked. It is the only line in this tree that is true.
  10. 1990sThe family on the domainRegisters tingsvad.dk, moves the mail in and inherits the spear, Mímir, and the tendency to decide later. Living. Still here.

Case file 6 · The network, then and now

The Tingsvad network

When the thing heard about ARPANET in 1969, it decided to build its own. Six runestones were connected with tarred rope, and each stone was given a small glowing screen that nobody knows the origin of. The network is in operation to this day. It can send exactly one message: adjourned. The message always arrives, but with a delay of between three days and four hundred years, depending on the fog.

The large stone in the middle is the server. The five small ones are clients. The stone with the sun wheel is the backup and has never been used, because nobody dares test it. The connection to the outside world runs through the river, which works as a kind of cable, with the limitation that it only carries data downstream.

In 2026 the network was connected to Cloudflare. It took a day to propagate. The thing found that fast. What the network became is described in the machine room.
